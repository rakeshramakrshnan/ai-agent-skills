# Appraise critically

Modes that judge the evidence rather than organize it. Both read from the corpus index. Cite specific papers and source locators throughout; preserve disagreement rather than smoothing it.

---

## agreement-map — where researchers agree, disagree, or partly agree

Organize the analysis around debated claims, not around individual papers.

Per debated claim:
- the claim
- papers supporting it
- papers challenging it
- the reason for the disagreement (differences in population, dataset, measurement, theory, method, time period, or context)
- the quality of the evidence on each side
- what a future study could do to resolve it

Look specifically for disagreement driven by those seven sources. End with the **5 disagreements most likely to produce a strong publishable study**, with a sentence each on why.

---

## methodology-audit — recurring methodological weaknesses

Audit the methods used across the corpus. Identify recurring weaknesses in: sample size, sampling bias, measurement quality, self-reported data, causal inference, statistical methods, replication, external validity, missing controls, dataset age, or experimental design.

Per recurring weakness:
- name the weakness
- cite examples from the papers (which papers exhibit it)
- propose a stronger methodological approach a new researcher could use

Report weaknesses that recur across multiple papers. A one-off limitation in a single paper is noted only if it bears on the corpus-level pattern. This is a cross-paper appraisal, not a formal risk-of-bias assessment unless the user supplies one.
